Intellectual Property & Data Protection

Protecting Intellectual Assets. Safeguarding Information.

Baba Law Firm advises domestic and international businesses on intellectual property rights, brand protection, technology-related agreements, personal data protection and privacy matters in Syria.

We assist companies, investors, entrepreneurs and rights holders in protecting, managing, licensing and enforcing their intellectual property, while addressing the legal requirements governing the collection, processing, use and transfer of personal data.

Our work covers intellectual property registration and commercialisation, contractual protection of confidential information, data protection compliance and disputes involving intellectual property and information-related rights.

Intellectual Property Protection in Syria

Securing Rights in Brands, Innovations and Creative Works.

Intellectual property protection in Syria is governed by several legislative instruments, including Law No. 8 of 2007 on Trademarks, Geographical Indications and Industrial Designs and Models, as amended, Law No. 18 of 2012 on Patents, and Legislative Decree No. 62 of 2013 on the Protection of Copyright and Related Rights.

These laws establish the legal framework for protecting trademarks, industrial designs, inventions, literary and artistic works, software and other forms of intellectual property, subject to the conditions and requirements applicable to each category.

Protection may involve registration, examination of prior rights, compliance with filing and renewal procedures, contractual arrangements governing ownership and use, and the enforcement of rights against infringement.

Syria is a party to the Paris Convention, the Madrid System for the international registration of marks, the Berne Convention and the Patent Cooperation Treaty. International businesses seeking protection in Syria should consider both the territorial nature of intellectual property rights and the international registration routes available to them.

We advise clients on intellectual property protection strategies, registration procedures, ownership arrangements and the legal measures available to protect their commercial and creative assets.

Personal Data Protection in Syria

Understanding Privacy and Data Processing Obligations.

The protection of personal data in Syria is principally governed by Law No. 12 of 2024 on the Protection of Electronic Personal Data, which applies to personal data processed wholly or partly by electronic means, and does not extend to data held solely in written or paper form.

The law establishes the Personal Data Protection Authority, which issues licences, permits and accreditations relating to the collection, storage, transfer and processing of personal data, and regulates the rights of data subjects and the obligations of controllers and processors.

As a general rule, personal data may be processed or disclosed only with the explicit consent of the data subject or in the cases permitted by law. Controllers and processors must obtain the applicable licence or permit from the Authority for activities involving personal data. Where the controller or processor is a legal entity, it must appoint a personal data protection officer in accordance with the requirements of the law.

Additional requirements apply to sensitive personal data, which includes health, biometric, genetic and financial data and data relating to children, as well as to electronic marketing. Cross-border transfers of personal data are subject to the approval of the Personal Data Protection Authority and the applicable requirements concerning the level of protection in the receiving country. Transfers to countries that do not meet the required level of protection may be permitted in specific circumstances, subject to the explicit consent of the data subject and the conditions prescribed by law.

We assist businesses in identifying their data protection obligations, reviewing internal procedures and contractual arrangements, and managing legal risks associated with the handling of personal information.

Data Protection for International Businesses

Addressing Local Obligations and Cross-Border Operations.

International businesses operating in Syria may process personal data relating to employees, customers and suppliers through systems involving more than one jurisdiction, including intra-group data sharing, external service providers and the transfer of personal data abroad.

Controllers and processors located outside Syria are required to appoint a representative in Syria, and making personal data available to a controller or processor abroad, including within a corporate group, requires a licence from the Authority and compliance with the conditions prescribed by law.

We advise businesses on the interaction between Syrian data protection requirements, foreign regimes that may apply to their activities, and their contractual arrangements and internal compliance policies.
