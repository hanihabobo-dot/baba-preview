## The International Presence in Syria
~ A Period of Return and Expansion.
Diplomatic missions are reopening in Damascus after years of absence, international organisations are expanding their programmes, and development institutions and international non-governmental organisations are moving from emergency response towards recovery and reconstruction. Each of them must re-establish premises, recruit and manage staff, contract with local suppliers and partners, and operate within a Syrian legal framework that has itself undergone significant change.
The status of diplomatic missions and international organisations is governed by the international conventions on diplomatic and consular relations, by headquarters, host country and cooperation agreements, and by Syrian law in the matters those instruments do not cover. International non-governmental organisations operate under a licence from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates, which also approves internationally funded cooperation projects implemented with local partners, after consulting the competent ministries and the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour. Local associations and NGOs are registered with and supervised by the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour, and may require sector-specific approvals for their activities. Internationally funded projects may be implemented only once approved, in coordination with the social affairs and technical directorates in the governorates concerned, and are subject to periodic and final evaluation.

## Key Legal Issues for Missions and Organisations
~ Operating with Certainty.
**Status, privileges and immunities.** The scope of immunities and of tax and customs exemptions, and their effect on contracts, employment, property and disputes, depends on the applicable conventions and agreements and must be considered in structuring every legal relationship.

**Premises and property.** Missions and organisations lease or acquire chanceries, offices, residences and warehouses, and must address title verification, lease terms, approvals for property held by foreign states or organisations, security works and the restoration of properties left unused for years.
**Locally engaged staff.** The employment of Syrian and resident staff raises questions of applicable law, social insurance, contract terms, termination and the resolution of employment claims, in light of the employer’s status and the protections available to employees.
**Registration and operations of NGOs.** International NGOs must obtain their licences, submit their work programmes and meet regular reporting requirements, and each internationally funded project implemented with local partners requires prior approval, through a route that depends on whether the international donor or partner is licensed. Local NGOs must complete their registration, obtain project approvals before implementation and submit periodic and final reports.
**Contracts, procurement and funds.** Supply, security, transport, construction and service contracts must reflect the organisation’s procurement rules and status, while banking arrangements, the transfer of funds and the protection of beneficiary data require particular attention.
