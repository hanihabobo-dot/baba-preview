## The Energy Sector in Syria
~ A Sector Opening to Private Investment.
Restoring reliable electricity supply is among the most pressing needs of the Syrian economy. Damage to generation, transmission and distribution assets, constraints on fuel supply and growing demand from households, industry and reconstruction have made the power sector a priority for public investment, international partnerships and private capital.
The sector is overseen by the Ministry of Energy, established in 2025 by merging the former ministries responsible for electricity, oil and mineral resources and water resources.
Under Decree No. 45 of 2026, the Syrian Electricity Company (SEC) was established as a wholly state-owned public holding company with legal personality and financial and administrative independence, reporting to the Minister of Energy. SEC succeeded the former public establishments responsible for electricity generation and for transmission and distribution, together with their affiliated companies, in their rights, obligations, contracts and agreements.
The Company is tasked with developing investment strategies for the sector, attracting domestic and international investors and entering into partnerships and joint ventures. Oil, gas and refining activities remain within the Ministry’s broader energy portfolio and are conducted through the relevant state-owned companies.
The electricity legislation, as amended in 2022, allows the Ministry to license private investors to build and operate conventional and renewable generation plants. The public grid operator is required to purchase the output of renewable plants procured through its own calls for offers, and may purchase other output at agreed prices. Licensed producers may also supply major and medium-voltage consumers directly, using the public network under a network use agreement, against network charges and within the network’s technical and available capacity, and renewable producers may supply low-voltage consumers through private networks. Larger projects may also be structured as public-private partnerships, concessions or investment projects licensed under the investment legislation. In November 2025, the Ministry of Energy signed final agreements with an international consortium for the construction and operation of large combined-cycle gas and solar generation plants, described as the first integrated public-private partnership in the sector.

Syria is also re-emerging as a transit route for regional energy trade. Since April 2026, Iraqi fuel oil and other petroleum products have been transported by road across Syrian territory for export through the port of Baniyas. In July 2026, the Syrian Petroleum Company signed a memorandum of understanding with Iraq’s Basra Oil Company on the rehabilitation of the Iraq–Syria crude oil pipeline to Baniyas, and a second memorandum with an international consortium for the related technical and financial studies; the implementation of the project remains subject to the conclusion of definitive agreements.

## Key Legal Issues in Energy Projects
~ From Licence to Commercial Operation.
**Licensing and market structure.** Each project must establish its regulatory basis: the generation licence, the permitted route to market, connection to the grid and the approvals required from the Ministry and other competent authorities.
**Independent power producer contracts.** An independent power project rests on a suite of interlocking agreements: the concession or implementation agreement with the State, the power purchase agreement with the public purchaser, fuel supply and transport, land, grid connection, construction and operation agreements, and the direct agreements required by lenders. Their bankability depends on a consistent allocation of risk across the suite, including the creditworthiness of the purchaser, capacity and energy payment structures, the currency of payment and its convertibility, tariff indexation, dispatch and curtailment risk, take-or-pay obligations, change in law and force majeure, termination payments, government support or guarantees and the transfer of the plant at the end of its term.
**Oil and gas exploration, production and refining.** Upstream activities have historically been carried out by state-owned companies and through production sharing and service contracts between the Syrian Petroleum Company and international oil companies, often implemented through joint operating companies. The rehabilitation of fields, gas processing facilities and refineries raises questions concerning the status of existing agreements, the negotiation of new contractual terms, cost recovery, the allocation of production and the restoration of damaged facilities.
**Fuel supply.** Thermal plants depend on secure arrangements for the supply and transport of gas or other fuel, often involving public suppliers and cross-border arrangements, and on the allocation of fuel supply risk between the project and the purchaser.
**Land, permits and environment.** Projects require secure rights over the site, including the allocation or lease of state-owned land, construction and environmental approvals and arrangements for transmission connections and rights of way.
**Financing and international participation.** Project finance requires security over project assets, contracts and accounts, the ability to transfer debt service and returns abroad, and compliance with the applicable trade, regulatory and financial requirements affecting the movement of equipment, contractors and funds.

**Transit, storage and export.** The movement of oil and petroleum products across Syria involves intergovernmental arrangements, customs transit procedures, port and terminal services, and the allocation of liability for loss, contamination or delay of cargo in transit. Transport and logistics companies participating in these operations typically require the appropriate transport licensing and must qualify with the Syrian Petroleum Company, demonstrating their legal standing, financial capacity, technical resources, safety and environmental systems and relevant experience, and providing financial guarantees for the shipments they handle.
**Contracts and disputes.** Engineering, procurement and construction contracts, equipment supply and operation and maintenance agreements must be aligned with the power purchase agreement, and disputes with public counterparties may involve the rules governing administrative contracts, jurisdiction and arbitration, depending on the legal nature of the contract and the applicable statutory requirements.
