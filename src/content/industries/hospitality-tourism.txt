## The Tourism Sector in Syria
~ A Destination Returning.
Few countries can match Syria’s concentration of historic cities, archaeological sites, religious heritage and Mediterranean coastline. Visitors are returning in growing numbers, led by the Syrian diaspora and visitors from the region, while the existing stock of hotels and tourism facilities requires substantial rehabilitation and expansion to meet demand and international standards.
The sector is overseen by the Ministry of Tourism, which licenses and classifies hotels, restaurants and other tourist establishments, regulates travel and tourism agencies and promotes tourism investment. The Ministry offers its own properties for investment through calls for offers and investment contracts, while tourism sites owned by other public entities are contracted by those entities, and qualifying projects may also be licensed under the investment legislation. Projects located in historic areas or near archaeological sites must also satisfy the heritage protection requirements of the competent authorities, while planning and building approvals are issued by local authorities.
Tourism investment is being encouraged through investment contracts for new hotels, resorts and leisure projects, the redevelopment of existing facilities and the incentives available to qualifying investment projects, and international hotel groups are assessing their return to the Syrian market.

## Key Legal Issues in Hospitality and Tourism
~ From Site to Guest.
**Investment structure and land.** Hotel and resort projects require secure rights over the site, whether through ownership, long-term lease or investment arrangements over publicly owned property, and a structure that addresses the respective roles of owner, developer, operator and financier.
**Licensing and classification.** Tourist establishments require licensing and classification, with standards that affect design, construction, operation and the services that may be offered.

**Operator and brand arrangements.** Hotel management, franchise and brand licensing agreements with international operators require careful negotiation of fees, performance standards, owner approvals, brand protection, termination and the effect of Syrian mandatory rules.
**Heritage and development constraints.** Projects in historic cities, near archaeological sites or on the coast may be subject to heritage protection, planning and environmental restrictions that shape what can be built and how.
**Operations.** Hospitality businesses must manage employment and social insurance obligations, consumer protection, health and safety, food safety and the protection of guest data, as well as supply and service contracts.
