## The Telecommunications Sector in Syria
~ A Sector Being Rebuilt.
The rehabilitation of Syria’s communications networks is a priority of the country’s recovery. Years of damage and underinvestment have left significant gaps in coverage, capacity and quality of service, while demand from households, businesses, public institutions and digital services continues to grow.
The sector is overseen by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology and regulated by the Syrian Telecommunications and Post Regulatory Authority, which grants licences, manages spectrum and numbering, sets technical and service obligations and supervises compliance. The state-owned Syrian Telecom remains the principal provider of fixed-line services, national transmission and international connectivity, alongside licensed mobile operators and internet service providers.
The sector is now opening to new investment. In June 2026, following an international bidding process, the Ministry announced the selection of a new mobile operator and signed an agreement for its entry into the market. A project for a national fibre-optic backbone, data centres and international connectivity has been awarded through a competitive tender and is being implemented through a joint venture with participation by a Syrian state fund, and the Ministry has invited investment in fibre-to-the-premises networks and shared indoor coverage infrastructure.

## Key Legal Issues in Telecommunications
~ From Licence to Network.
**Licensing and spectrum.** Telecommunications services and networks require licences from the competent authority, with conditions concerning coverage, quality of service, spectrum use, licence fees and revenue sharing, and with restrictions on the transfer of licences and changes of control.
**Market entry and ownership.** Foreign operators and investors must determine the appropriate investment vehicle, address any applicable conditions on ownership, and structure arrangements that may involve participation by public entities, local partners or the transfer of assets and subscribers from existing operators.

**Infrastructure and rights of way.** Network deployment depends on access to sites, towers, ducts and rights of way, permits from local authorities, infrastructure sharing arrangements and agreements with the national transmission provider.
**Equipment and technology supply.** Network equipment, software and services must satisfy type approval and customs requirements, and international supply arrangements must be structured in compliance with the applicable trade and regulatory requirements.
**Interconnection and commercial arrangements.** Interconnection, roaming, wholesale capacity, content and value-added service agreements, and arrangements with agents and distributors shape the economics of operators and service providers.
**Data, security and consumer obligations.** Operators are subject to obligations under the telecommunications legislation and their licence conditions concerning the confidentiality of communications, information security, cooperation with the competent authorities and the protection of subscribers, together with the personal data protection legislation where it applies to their processing activities.
