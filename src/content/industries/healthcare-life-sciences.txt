## The Healthcare Sector in Syria
~ Restoring Capacity and Access.
Syria’s healthcare system faces the task of restoring damaged facilities, rebuilding its workforce and meeting the needs of a population with growing demand for specialised care. The National Health Strategic Plan for 2026–2028, launched by the Ministry of Health in December 2025, sets out priorities for rebuilding the health system, expanding access to care, strengthening regulatory oversight and supporting domestic pharmaceutical production, alongside digital transformation of health services.
The sector combines public hospitals and health centres with a substantial private sector of hospitals, clinics, laboratories, diagnostic centres and pharmacies. Syria also has an established domestic pharmaceutical industry, while imported medicines, medical devices and equipment meet needs that local production does not cover.
The Ministry of Health licenses healthcare facilities, regulates the registration, pricing, importation and distribution of medicines and medical products, and oversees standards of care. Healthcare professionals practise under the supervision of their professional syndicates, and health insurance is subject to the oversight of the insurance regulator. In 2025 and 2026, the Ministry of Health updated its rules on the registration of foreign manufacturers and their products, with separate requirements for medicines and biological products and for medical devices and supplies.

## Key Legal Issues in Healthcare and Life Sciences
~ From Licence to Patient.
**Facility licensing and investment.** Hospitals, specialised centres, laboratories and diagnostic facilities require licensing and must meet technical, staffing and operational requirements, while investors must structure ownership, management and professional responsibilities within the rules governing medical practice.

**Medicines and medical products.** Placing medicines, biological products, medical devices and supplies on the Syrian market typically involves, depending on the product category, the licensing of a local importer or warehouse, the registration of the foreign manufacturer through an authorised local representative, and the registration of each product on the basis of the documents required for the product concerned. Registrations must be renewed before they expire, and products remain subject to pricing, import and distribution requirements.
**Manufacturing and technology transfer.** Pharmaceutical manufacturers and their international partners must address manufacturing licences and quality standards, licensing and toll-manufacturing arrangements, and the protection of trademarks, know-how and other intellectual property.
**Procurement and supply.** Supplying public hospitals and health programmes involves public procurement procedures, tender requirements and contract performance obligations.
**Patient data and liability.** Health information is sensitive personal data whose collection, processing and transfer require particular care, and providers must manage informed consent, medical liability and professional insurance.
