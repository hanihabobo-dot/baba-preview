## The Aviation Sector in Syria
~ A Market Reopening to the World.
After more than a decade in which Syrian airspace was considered high-risk and international connections were sharply curtailed, Syria’s aviation sector is being rebuilt. International carriers have resumed flights to Damascus and Aleppo, new Syrian operators are being certified, regional airports are being rehabilitated, and major investments are under way in airport development and air navigation systems.
The sector is regulated by the General Authority of Civil Aviation and Air Transport, established in 2025 as an independent authority attached to the Presidency, which succeeded the former civil aviation establishment and the air transport directorate of the Ministry of Transport. The Authority is responsible for air safety, security and service standards, the organisation of airport and air navigation infrastructure and the certification of air operators, and represents Syria in international aviation bodies. The national carrier has been reorganised as a state-owned holding company, and Syria’s reintegration into the international aviation system is supported by cooperation with the International Civil Aviation Organization and by the conclusion of air transport agreements with other states.
Airport development is increasingly carried out through investment and concession arrangements with international consortia, while Syrian and foreign operators compete in a market expected to grow with the return of the diaspora, business travel and tourism.

## Key Legal Issues in Aviation
~ From Operating Rights to Aircraft on the Ground.
**Market entry and operating rights.** Syrian operators require an air operator certificate and must satisfy the authority’s regulatory, technical and operational requirements, while foreign carriers operate on the basis of air transport agreements, designations and operating permits.

**Aircraft acquisition, leasing and finance.** Aircraft purchase, lease and finance transactions require attention to registration, the creation and enforcement of security, deregistration and export, repossession rights under applicable law, and the compliance requirements of manufacturers, lessors, financiers and insurers.
**Airports and infrastructure.** Airport development, terminal operation, ground handling, fuel supply and commercial concessions are structured through investment, concession and service agreements with public entities, raising questions of exclusivity, revenue sharing, performance obligations and termination.
**Aviation services.** Ground handling, maintenance, catering, cargo, training and travel distribution activities are subject to licensing and approval requirements, and to commercial arrangements with airlines and airport operators.
**International compliance.** Aircraft transactions, spare parts, maintenance services, insurance and financing require assessment of applicable sanctions, export control requirements and international aviation standards, including restrictions that may affect the delivery, operation and servicing of aircraft.
**Liability and insurance.** Carriers and service providers must manage liability towards passengers, shippers and third parties, aviation insurance requirements and claims arising from delay, damage or loss.
