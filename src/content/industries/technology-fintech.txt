## The Technology Sector in Syria
~ A Digital Economy Taking Shape.
Syria has a growing community of software developers, engineers and entrepreneurs, many of whom have built their skills serving regional and international clients, alongside a diaspora with deep ties to technology markets abroad. Demand for digital services is rising across commerce, banking, government services, education and logistics.
The lifting of international economic sanctions on Syria has removed many of the legal barriers that had cut Syrian technology businesses off from international cloud services, developer tools, application platforms and payment networks, creating opportunities to reconnect with them and for international providers to enter the market, although access in practice remains subject to applicable legal requirements and the policies of individual service providers. At the same time, the authorities are modernising the legal framework for the sector, including the regulation of electronic payments and electronic money, the protection of personal data and the expansion of digital public services.
Technology businesses deal with several regulators, depending on their activities: the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology and the telecommunications regulator for networks and internet services, the Central Bank of Syria for payments and financial services, the Personal Data Protection Authority established under the personal data protection legislation for the processing of personal data, and the competent authorities for electronic signatures and consumer protection.

## Key Legal Issues for Technology Businesses
~ From Founding to Scale.
**Founding and investment structures.** Start-ups must choose a corporate form that can accommodate founders, employees and investors. Venture investment terms, such as preferred rights, vesting and employee share incentives, need to be adapted to the corporate forms available under Syrian law or implemented through an appropriate holding structure.

**Cross-border services and revenues.** Syrian technology companies serving clients abroad, and international providers serving Syrian customers, must address the contractual framework, the receipt and transfer of payments, foreign exchange requirements and the tax treatment of cross-border services.
**Financial technology.** Regulated payment, electronic money and other financial services activities may require licensing or approval under the Central Bank of Syria’s framework, and partnerships between technology companies and banks must be structured in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements.
**Data and security.** The collection, processing, hosting and cross-border transfer of personal data, including through cloud services located outside Syria, must be assessed against applicable data protection, consent, authorisation and information security requirements.
**Intellectual property and talent.** The value of a technology business depends on the ownership of its software, data and brands, which requires effective assignment of rights from founders, employees and contractors and the registration of trademarks in Syria.
