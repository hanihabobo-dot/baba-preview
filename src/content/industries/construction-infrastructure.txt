## The Construction Sector in Syria
~ A Sector Central to Recovery.
Syria’s need for reconstruction and the rehabilitation of infrastructure, housing, utilities and public facilities places the construction sector at the centre of the country’s economic recovery. Projects are undertaken by public authorities and state-owned companies, by private developers and investors, and increasingly through structures involving international contractors, financiers and development partners.
Construction activity is regulated at several levels. The practice of contracting is organised through the Syrian Contractors Syndicate under the law governing the profession, and contractors are classified, under the classification system issued by the Ministry of Public Works and Housing, in categories that determine the works they may undertake. The Engineers Syndicate regulates engineering offices and the supervision of works; governorates and municipalities issue building permits and enforce planning and building regulations; and the Syrian Investment Authority licenses qualifying investment projects. Where the employer is a public entity, the project is also governed by the public contracting rules, which prescribe tendering procedures, guarantees and the employer’s contractual powers, and the resulting contract may qualify as an administrative contract.
International contractors entering the market must establish an appropriate local presence, meet classification and registration requirements and adapt international contracting practice, including standard forms such as FIDIC, to Syrian law and local conditions.

## Key Legal Issues in Construction Projects
~ From Tender to Final Account.
**Public-sector contracting.** Projects for ministries, public companies and local authorities are tendered and performed under rules that fix the form of the contract, the guarantees required and the employer’s powers of variation, penalty and termination. Pricing in conditions of currency and cost volatility, extensions of time and the recovery of additional costs require careful attention to the contract and the applicable rules.

**Market entry and qualification.** Foreign contractors and consultants must address branch registration or the formation of a local entity, contractor classification, joint venture or consortium arrangements with Syrian partners and the registration of engineering and consultancy services.
**Risk allocation in private projects.** Construction contracts between private parties must allocate design responsibility, site conditions, delay, payment security and the currency of payment within the mandatory rules of Syrian law, including the decennial liability of contractors and engineers for structural defects.
**Equipment, materials and labour.** Projects depend on the importation of machinery and materials under the customs regime, on the employment of local and foreign personnel in compliance with labour, social insurance and work permit requirements, and on project insurance arrangements.
**Claims and disputes.** Delays, disruption, variations, defective works, payment defaults and termination give rise to claims that may be resolved by negotiation, engineer’s determination, arbitration or litigation, with disputes arising from contracts qualifying as administrative contracts subject to the jurisdiction of the competent Council of State courts, and arbitration subject to the applicable statutory requirements.
