## The Industrial and Trade Sector in Syria
~ A Productive Base in Recovery.
Syria has a long industrial and trading tradition, with established manufacturing in textiles, food processing, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, construction materials and engineering industries, and a geographical position that has historically made it a trade route between the Mediterranean, the Gulf and Europe. Many industrial facilities have resumed operations, and new investment in production lines and industrial zones is under way.
Industrial activity is overseen by the Ministry of Economy and Industry, which is responsible for industrial licensing, the industrial cities and trade policy. The industrial cities allocate land and grant licences for manufacturing investment, while the free zones, administered by the General Establishment for Free Zones under the General Authority for Border Crossings and Customs, provide arrangements for storage, processing and re-export.
Trade is undergoing significant reform. Industrial licensing procedures have been simplified, the importation of production equipment has been facilitated, and a new customs decree and harmonised customs tariff were issued in 2026, while the lifting of international economic sanctions has reopened access to international suppliers, markets and trade finance.

## Key Legal Issues for Manufacturers and Traders
~ Along the Supply Chain.
**Industrial licensing and location.** Establishing or expanding a facility requires industrial licensing and the appropriate site, whether in an industrial city, a free zone or another permitted location, together with environmental, safety and utility approvals.
**Import and export.** Importers and exporters must address customs classification, valuation and origin, import and export requirements for regulated goods, temporary admission and free zone procedures, and conformity with Syrian standards and labelling requirements.

**Distribution and agency.** Foreign manufacturers selling into Syria must structure their relationships with local agents and distributors, including registration, exclusivity, termination and the compensation that may be claimed on termination.
**Payment and trade finance.** International sales depend on secure payment terms, documentary credits and guarantees, and on compliance with foreign exchange and transfer requirements.
**Product compliance and liability.** Manufacturers and distributors must comply with standards, labelling, consumer protection and competition rules, and manage contractual and product liability towards buyers and consumers.
