## Real Estate Ownership and Transactions in Syria
~ Understanding Property Rights and Legal Requirements.
Real estate ownership and transactions in Syria are governed by a legal framework that includes the Syrian Civil Code (Legislative Decree No. 84 of 1949), the real estate registration system established under Decision No. 188 of 1926, the Rent Law (Law No. 20 of 2015), as amended, and the laws governing the ownership of property by non-Syrians.
Property transactions may involve verification of registered ownership, examination of encumbrances and third-party rights, compliance with registration formalities, and assessment of restrictions affecting the transfer or use of property.
Ownership of real estate by non-Syrians is generally regulated by Law No. 11 of 2011, as amended, which establishes specific restrictions and approval requirements. Investment projects may also benefit from provisions under Investment Law No. 18 of 2021, as amended, concerning the acquisition or leasing of property required for their activities.
Depending on the property and its intended use, additional requirements may arise in relation to zoning, building permits, land use and planning regulations, and approvals from competent authorities. Land within approved urban plans may generally be transferred freely, subject to Legislative Decree No. 26 of 2013, which restricts the transfer, before development, of land acquired from public entities or held by housing cooperatives and renders transactions concluded in breach of those restrictions void.

## Real Estate Development
~ The Legal and Institutional Framework.
Real estate development in Syria is governed by Investment Law No. 18 of 2021, as amended by Law No. 2 of 2023 and Decree No. 114 of 2025, together with the applicable executive instructions and other regulatory requirements.
Law No. 2 of 2023 abolished the former General Authority for Real Estate Development and Investment and transferred its functions to the Syrian Investment Authority, which is responsible for the regulation and supervision of the sector.
Under the applicable framework, a real estate development project requires the establishment of a development zone by decision of the Supreme Council for Economic Development, upon the proposal of the Syrian Investment Authority, and the participation of a developer duly licensed by the Authority.
Real estate development may involve specialised corporate structures, land-sharing and joint development agreements, contractual arrangements with landowners or public entities, and regulatory approvals relating to planning, infrastructure and project implementation.
Off-plan sales and associated escrow arrangements are regulated by Law No. 25 of 2011, as amended, which establishes approval requirements, safeguards for purchasers and penalties for certain violations. Escrow accounts for real estate development projects may be opened only in accordance with the procedures and approvals required by the Syrian Investment Authority and the applicable banking regulations.
We advise developers, investors, landowners, purchasers and public entities on developer licensing, development zone procedures, land-sharing arrangements, off-plan sales requirements, development agreements and the legal structuring of real estate projects.

## Real Estate Due Diligence and Transaction Risk
~ Verifying Rights Before Commitment.
Real estate transactions require careful assessment of the legal status of the property and the rights of the parties involved.
In Syria, particular risks may arise from unregistered or informally held property, transactions based on powers of attorney or court judgments rather than registered transfers, competing claims to the same property, discrepancies between physical possession and registered title, and property records affected by the years of conflict.
We assist clients in identifying legal risks, reviewing transaction documentation and addressing title and registration issues before completing property acquisitions or development arrangements.
