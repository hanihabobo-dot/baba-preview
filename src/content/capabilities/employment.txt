## Employment Law in Syria
~ Understanding Employer Obligations and Employee Rights.
Employment relationships in Syria are principally governed by Labour Law No. 17 of 2010, as amended, together with the Social Insurance Law (Law No. 92 of 1959), as amended, implementing regulations and other relevant legal provisions.
The Labour Law regulates employment contracts, wages, working hours, leave entitlements, occupational health and safety, disciplinary measures, collective labour relations, termination of employment and the resolution of labour disputes.
Its provisions apply principally to employment in the private sector. Employees of the state and public entities may be subject to the Basic Law for State Employees (Law No. 50 of 2004), as amended, or other applicable employment legislation, depending on the nature of their employment.
The law establishes minimum protections for employees and imposes obligations on employers concerning employment documentation, working conditions and statutory entitlements. Contractual provisions and internal employment policies must be assessed in light of these mandatory requirements.
We advise employers on the interpretation and application of Syrian employment legislation, with particular attention to contractual compliance, employment-related liabilities and the prevention of disputes.

## Employment Termination and Dispute Prevention
~ Managing Decisions and Reducing Legal Exposure.
The termination of an employment relationship may give rise to significant legal and financial consequences, particularly where statutory procedures, notice requirements or employee entitlements have not been properly addressed.
Under the Labour Law, the consequences of termination differ according to whether the contract is for a fixed or an indefinite term, the grounds relied upon and compliance with the applicable procedures. Unlawful termination may give rise to statutory compensation and other remedies, as provided under applicable law.
We assist employers in assessing termination risks, reviewing supporting documentation, determining potential liabilities and structuring employment separations in accordance with applicable law.

## International Employers in Syria
~ Aligning Global Practices with Local Requirements.
International companies operating in Syria often employ staff under arrangements that combine local and foreign elements, including secondments from a parent company, dual employment contracts and contracts governed by foreign law.
Mandatory provisions of Syrian labour and social insurance legislation may apply to work performed in Syria regardless of the law chosen by the parties, subject to applicable conflict-of-laws rules. Investment projects licensed under the Investment Law may also be subject to specific provisions concerning the employment of foreign personnel.
We assist international employers in structuring employment arrangements, aligning group policies with Syrian requirements and managing the legal risks associated with expatriate and local staff.
