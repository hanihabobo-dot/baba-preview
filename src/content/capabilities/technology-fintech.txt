## Technology and Digital Business in Syria
~ Structuring Digital Operations Within the Legal Framework.
Technology-related activities in Syria are governed by a combination of commercial, contractual and sector-specific legislation, including Law No. 4 of 2009 on Electronic Signatures and Network Services and Law No. 3 of 2014 on Electronic Transactions, together with the applicable regulations governing telecommunications, consumer protection and other regulated activities.
The legal requirements applicable to a digital business depend on the nature of its services, the contractual relationships involved and whether its activities fall within a regulated sector.
Electronic contracts, digital signatures, electronic records and electronic payments must be assessed against the conditions established by this legislation, including the evidentiary requirements applicable to electronic writing and certified electronic signatures. Certain transactions may remain subject to specific formalities and cannot necessarily be completed through electronic means alone.
Businesses providing digital services must also consider contractual liability, the allocation of operational responsibilities, customer-facing terms, information security, personal data protection under Law No. 12 of 2024, and compliance with the regulatory requirements applicable to their activities.
We assist clients in structuring technology-related operations, preparing contractual documentation and assessing the legal implications of delivering products and services through digital channels.

## Financial Technology and Electronic Payments
~ Understanding Licensing and Regulatory Requirements.
Financial technology activities in Syria operate within the banking and financial regulatory framework, including Law No. 23 of 2002 on the Central Bank of Syria and the Basic Monetary System, as amended, and the rules applicable to payment and financial services.
Decision No. 1124 of 18 August 2026 of the General Secretariat of the Presidency of the Republic approved the Electronic Payment and Transfer System in Syria, establishing a regulatory framework for payment service providers, electronic money service providers and operators of electronic payment and transfer systems.
The framework addresses licensing and regulatory approvals, payment instruments, electronic money, customer protection, operational requirements and oversight by the Central Bank of Syria. It also provides for a regulatory sandbox through which innovative financial activities may be tested under the Central Bank’s supervision, subject to applicable conditions.
Decision No. 616 of 20 September 2026 of the Management Committee of the Central Bank of Syria approved the implementing instructions concerning licensing requirements and procedures, capital requirements, financial guarantees and the regularisation of existing activities.
Depending on the nature and scope of the services, applicable requirements may also include the safeguarding of customer funds, operational controls, arrangements with agents and outsourced technology providers, and conditions applicable to cross-border payment arrangements.
We advise clients on the legal and regulatory considerations involved in establishing, licensing and operating financial technology and electronic payment businesses in Syria, including the regularisation of existing activities under the new framework.

## International Technology Businesses in Syria
~ Aligning Cross-Border Services with Local Requirements.
International technology companies entering the Syrian market may operate through local subsidiaries, registered branches, licensing arrangements or contractual relationships with Syrian customers and service providers. These arrangements require consideration of sector-specific licensing, payment arrangements, data protection requirements and applicable restrictions on cross-border transactions.
We assist international businesses in evaluating market-entry structures, negotiating technology-related agreements, including governing law and dispute resolution provisions, and aligning their operations with applicable Syrian legal and regulatory requirements.
