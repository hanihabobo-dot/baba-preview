## Regulatory Compliance in Syria
~ Understanding the Legal and Regulatory Framework.
Businesses operating in Syria are subject to regulatory requirements arising from commercial, financial, investment and sector-specific legislation, together with decisions and instructions issued by the competent authorities.
Depending on the nature of their activities, companies may be required to obtain licences, maintain regulatory registrations, comply with reporting obligations and satisfy requirements relating to foreign exchange, anti-money laundering, import and export activities, and other regulated operations.
The applicable requirements may differ according to the company’s legal form, ownership structure, business activities and the sector in which it operates.
Foreign companies and investors may also need to consider compliance obligations arising under the laws of their home jurisdictions or the jurisdictions through which their transactions are conducted.
We advise clients on identifying the regulatory requirements applicable to their activities and on addressing compliance issues that may affect their operations, transactions and contractual relationships.

## International Sanctions
~ Economic Sanctions on Syria Have Been Lifted.
The United States, the European Union and the United Kingdom have lifted their comprehensive economic sanctions on Syria, marking a new chapter in the country’s international economic relations.
Syria and the Syrian Government are no longer subject to the general economic sanctions previously imposed on them. These developments have restored significant opportunities for international trade, foreign investment, banking relationships and economic cooperation.
The principal measures lifting these sanctions are:

- **United States** — Executive Order 14312 of 30 June 2025, revoking the Syria sanctions programme with effect from 1 July 2025; the repeal of the Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act by the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2026, signed on 18 December 2025; and the rescission of Syria’s designation as a State Sponsor of Terrorism on 24 August 2026.
- **European Union** — Council Decision (CFSP) 2025/1096 and Council Regulation (EU) 2025/1098 of 27 May 2025, lifting the economic restrictive measures applied to Syria.
- **United Kingdom** — The Syria (Sanctions) (EU Exit) (Amendment) Regulations 2025 (SI 2025/507), in force from 25 April 2025, and The Syria (Sanctions) (EU Exit) (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/436), in force from 22 April 2026, removing the trade, financial and investment restrictions on Syria.
The lifting of economic sanctions represents a fundamental change for international businesses and investors. Syria is once again open to commercial partnerships, investment projects, financial transactions and long-term business development.
As with other international markets, businesses should undertake standard counterparty screening and comply with applicable international trade requirements.
Baba Law Firm supports international investors and businesses in taking advantage of these opportunities, providing legal advice on market entry, investment structuring, regulatory and compliance requirements and commercial transactions in Syria.
